1. What is the Solow model?

A simple model of how a country's capital (K: machines, factories) and output (Y) change over time. Each year the country produces Y = A√K, invests a share s of it (I = sY), and some capital wears out (D = δK).

If more is invested than wears out, capital grows. If less, it shrinks. When they are equal, the economy settles: the steady state.
  • A is technology: higher A means the same capital makes more output.
  • √K means diminishing returns: with A = 2.60, K = 100 gives Y = 26, but K = 400 gives only Y = 52.

6. What if AI makes technology 20% better?

Better technology makes every machine more productive, so the economy settles higher. A is squared in the formulas, so +20% in A gives 1.2 × 1.2 = 1.44, which is +44%.

7. What if the country saves more?

Saving more raises the level of capital and output, but not growth forever: growth only speeds up while moving to the new steady state. Consumption is highest at s = 0.50.

9. Summary

  • Steady state: where the economy stops changing.
  • I = D there: new capital exactly replaces worn-out capital.
  • Better technology: each unit of capital produces more, so K*, Y* and C* are higher.
  • +20% A gives +44% Y*: Y* = sA²/δ, and 1.2² = 1.44.
  • Saving more: raises the level, not long-run growth, because of diminishing returns. Only ongoing technological progress sustains growth.
